# Depth Guide

Standards, article shapes, and failure modes for deep-summary articles.

## What "depth" means

Four layers, each deeper than the last. A deep article must reach at least
layer 3, and ideally ends on layer 4:

1. **Compression** — what the text says.
2. **Reconstruction** — how it argues: structure, evidence, method.
3. **Situatedness** — why it matters: assumptions, context, implications,
   relation to neighboring ideas.
4. **Critique** — whether it holds: strengths, weaknesses, open questions,
   your own position.

## Recommended article shapes

### Shape A: Argument-led (default)

1. **Hook / introduction** — the question the text answers and why anyone
   should care.
2. **Core thesis** — the thesis restated sharply, including the author's
   key conceptual move.
3. **Argument breakdown** — one section per major argument:
   claim → evidence → mechanism → assessment.
4. **Context and significance** — where this sits among related ideas;
   what changes if the thesis is true.
5. **Critical assessment** — tensions, missing evidence, the strongest
   counterargument, and your position.
6. **Conclusion** — what to take away; what to read or do next.

### Shape B: Question-led

For texts that respond to a controversy or survey a problem: organize the
article around 3–5 questions the text answers. Each section = the question
→ the text's answer → how convincing, and why.

### Shape C: Mechanism-led

For technical or how-to texts: trace one central mechanism end to end
(motivation → setup → how it actually works → where it breaks → what it
enables), weaving the text's evidence into each stage.

## Common failure modes

- **List-ification**: bullets replace prose → rewrite as paragraphs; keep
  lists only for genuinely enumerable support.
- **Paraphrase spiral**: restating without explaining → add the "why"
  behind every "what".
- **Uncritical praise or dismissal**: verdict without engagement → state
  the strongest version of the author's view before challenging it.
- **Fabrication**: content not present in the source → every factual claim
  must be traceable; personal judgments must be visibly marked as such.
- **Ending fade**: conclusion merely repeats the introduction → close with
  implications, open questions, or a concrete next step instead.

## Quality checklist (run before delivering)

- [ ] A reader who has not seen the source understands both the thesis and
      the argument for it
- [ ] Every major claim is grounded in a specific passage, figure, or data
      point
- [ ] At least one non-obvious insight beyond the text's own framing
- [ ] The critique takes a position and defends it
- [ ] Prose is continuous; lists only support, never replace, paragraphs
- [ ] Language and length match the output requirements
